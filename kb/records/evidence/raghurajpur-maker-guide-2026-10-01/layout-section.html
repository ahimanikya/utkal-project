01 · A closer look

The story on the cloth

Start at the edge of a painting. Follow a repeated motif, then let your eye travel towards the figures. Odisha Tourism connects Raghurajpur’s Pattachitra with Jagannath traditions and stories from the epics. Ask the maker where this particular story begins: a picture can become something you read together.

02 · A closer look

Before the picture, the surface

The Government of Odisha describes a traditional cloth-painting process. Use these three stages as things to look for, then ask the artist how their own practice differs.

  1. 01

    Look for the ground

    Prepare the cloth

    Chalk and tamarind-seed glue help make the cloth ready to take paint. Ask whether you may see an unpainted surface.

  2. 02

    Follow an edge

    Build the picture

    The account places the border first, followed by brush drawing and flat colour. Notice how an edge holds the scene together.

  3. 03

    Stay with a detail

    Read the fine line

    Fine dark outlines bring details into focus. Choose a small figure and ask which marks give it expression.

03 · A closer look

A craft that found another future

A December 2005 Orissa Review account recalls a difficult period for the painters and a mid-twentieth-century revival. Citing dance historian Ileana Citaristi, it connects renewed opportunities around 1953 with Halina Zealey’s involvement. Read this as a published historical account, not a complete history of the village. The makers’ work is the thread that carries it forward.

04 · A closer look

More than a painting village

The village’s cultural life also moves beyond the painted surface. Official accounts connect Raghurajpur with Gotipua, a dance tradition related to the development of Odissi, and identify it as Kelucharan Mohapatra’s birthplace. If dance draws you here, ask a school or organiser about an invited visit or announced performance. Training is someone’s working day too; a village visit is not a performance booking.

05 · A closer look

A doorway into someone’s working day

Writing about his 2019 visit for Odisha Tourism, Biswa Prakash describes artists explaining their work and painting on surfaces beyond cloth, including coconuts and bottles. His account offers a way to begin: ask how an artist chooses a surface, and how the story changes with it. It does not promise that a workshop will be open when you arrive.

06 · A closer look

Meet a maker, with permission

Begin with a greeting and ask whether this is a good time. If you would like a demonstration, agree its length and fee before it starts. Ask who made a piece, which scene it tells and how the colours were chosen. Request permission separately for photographs of people, artwork and the home; accept a refusal without pressing.

07 · A closer look

Bring a story home

Choose a piece you want to live with, not just carry away. Ask for the maker’s preferred name, materials, price, packing and care advice, and keep a receipt. If work is commissioned, agree the design, delivery and payment terms in writing. Buying a painting does not itself give permission to reproduce the artwork. Record the story only as its teller wishes it to be shared.

Before you go

Odisha Tourism places Raghurajpur about 12 km from Puri. Arrange the meeting point and return journey, then agree your visit with a maker. Leave time to look at one piece slowly; a village visit does not promise an open workshop or a dance performance.

From cloth to a story

The traditional process described by the Government of Odisha starts with a prepared cloth ground, then a border, brush drawing, flat colours and fine outlines. Ask which stage you are seeing and which materials this maker uses. A published tradition does not describe every contemporary piece.

A workshop is a working place

Agree the appointment, group size, demonstration time and fee with the maker. Ask before photographing people, art or a home. For a purchase, keep the maker’s preferred credit, materials, care advice and receipt; agree commission and delivery terms separately. Buying work does not itself grant reproduction permission.

Make room for dance, by arrangement

Raghurajpur is associated with Gotipua and is Kelucharan Mohapatra’s birthplace. Ask a school or organiser whether an invited visit or announced performance is available. Confirm timing, fee and photography separately; do not interrupt training or assume a show is included.

Check the path and the way back

Ask your host about the exact entrance, walking surface, steps, seating, shade and toilets. Agree pickup, waiting and return arrangements with your transport provider. Named makers, appointments, fees, accessible routes and current facilities have not been verified by Utkal.

Six questions to take with you

Ask the people arranging your visit. These prompts help you prepare; they do not confirm local arrangements.

Access
Is the workshop I hope to visit welcoming visitors that day, and should I arrange a time first?
Getting there and back
How will I arrive without blocking homes or workspaces, and how will I arrange my return?
Comfort and pace
How long would the maker like us to stay, and what pace lets us listen without interrupting their work?
Food and water
Where will I plan refreshments separately so a workshop visit does not assume hospitality from a household?
People and pictures
Before recording a person, process or artwork, have I asked permission and how they would like to be credited?
A change of plan
If a maker is busy, can I return at an agreed time or choose another welcome visit without pressing them?
