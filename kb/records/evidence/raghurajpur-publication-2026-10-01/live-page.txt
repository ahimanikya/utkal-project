    - heading "The story on the cloth" [level=2]
    - paragraph: "Start at the edge of a painting. Follow a repeated motif, then let your eye travel towards the figures. Odisha Tourism connects Raghurajpur’s Pattachitra with Jagannath traditions and stories from the epics. Ask the maker where this particular story begins: a picture can become something you read together."
    - paragraph: 02 · A closer look
    - heading "Before the picture, the surface" [level=2]
    - paragraph: The Government of Odisha describes a traditional cloth-painting process. Use these three stages as things to look for, then ask the artist how their own practice differs.
    - list "Three stages to notice":

      - heading "Prepare the cloth" [level=3]
      - paragraph: Chalk and tamarind-seed glue help make the cloth ready to take paint. Ask whether you may see an unpainted surface.

      - paragraph: The account places the border first, followed by brush drawing and flat colour. Notice how an edge holds the scene together.

      - heading "Read the fine line" [level=3]
      - paragraph: Fine dark outlines bring details into focus. Choose a small figure and ask which marks give it expression.
    - paragraph: 03 · A closer look
    - heading "A craft that found another future" [level=2]
    - paragraph: A December 2005 Orissa Review account recalls a difficult period for the painters and a mid-twentieth-century revival. Citing dance historian Ileana Citaristi, it connects renewed opportunities around 1953 with Halina Zealey’s involvement. Read this as a published historical account, not a complete history of the village. The makers’ work is the thread that carries it forward.
    - paragraph: 04 · A closer look
    - heading "More than a painting village" [level=2]
    - paragraph: The village’s cultural life also moves beyond the painted surface. Official accounts connect Raghurajpur with Gotipua, a dance tradition related to the development of Odissi, and identify it as Kelucharan Mohapatra’s birthplace. If dance draws you here, ask a school or organiser about an invited visit or announced performance. Training is someone’s working day too; a village visit is not a performance booking.
    - paragraph: 05 · A closer look
    - heading "A doorway into someone’s working day" [level=2]
    - paragraph: "Writing about his 2019 visit for Odisha Tourism, Biswa Prakash describes artists explaining their work and painting on surfaces beyond cloth, including coconuts and bottles. His account offers a way to begin: ask how an artist chooses a surface, and how the story changes with it. It does not promise that a workshop will be open when you arrive."
    - paragraph: 06 · A closer look
    - heading "Meet a maker, with permission" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Begin with a greeting and ask whether this is a good time. If you would like a demonstration, agree its length and fee before it starts. Ask who made a piece, which scene it tells and how the colours were chosen. Request permission separately for photographs of people, artwork and the home; accept a refusal without pressing.
    - paragraph: 07 · A closer look
    - heading "Bring a story home" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Choose a piece you want to live with, not just carry away. Ask for the maker’s preferred name, materials, price, packing and care advice, and keep a receipt. If work is commissioned, agree the design, delivery and payment terms in writing. Buying a painting does not itself give permission to reproduce the artwork. Record the story only as its teller wishes it to be shared.
    - paragraph: 08 · A closer look
    - heading "Plan the practical details" [level=2]
    - paragraph: The palms and water in this older photograph show a setting, not a workshop entrance. Odisha Tourism places Raghurajpur about 12 km from Puri. Arrange an exact meeting point and your return before setting out; distance alone cannot tell you the journey time. Ask your host about the path, steps, shade, toilets and a place to sit. No named host or performance has been booked or inspected by Utkal.

      - heading "Before you go" [level=3]
      - paragraph: Odisha Tourism places Raghurajpur about 12 km from Puri. Arrange the meeting point and return journey, then agree your visit with a maker. Leave time to look at one piece slowly; a village visit does not promise an open workshop or a dance performance.
      - heading "From cloth to a story" [level=3]
      - paragraph: The traditional process described by the Government of Odisha starts with a prepared cloth ground, then a border, brush drawing, flat colours and fine outlines. Ask which stage you are seeing and which materials this maker uses. A published tradition does not describe every contemporary piece.
      - heading "A workshop is a working place" [level=3]
      - paragraph: Agree the appointment, group size, demonstration time and fee with the maker. Ask before photographing people, art or a home. For a purchase, keep the maker’s preferred credit, materials, care advice and receipt; agree commission and delivery terms separately. Buying work does not itself grant reproduction permission.
      - heading "Make room for dance, by arrangement" [level=3]
      - paragraph: Raghurajpur is associated with Gotipua and is Kelucharan Mohapatra’s birthplace. Ask a school or organiser whether an invited visit or announced performance is available. Confirm timing, fee and photography separately; do not interrupt training or assume a show is included.
      - heading "Check the path and the way back" [level=3]
      - paragraph: Ask your host about the exact entrance, walking surface, steps, seating, shade and toilets. Agree pickup, waiting and return arrangements with your transport provider. Named makers, appointments, fees, accessible routes and current facilities have not been verified by Utkal.
