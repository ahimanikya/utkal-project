# From population to a place’s voices

**Ahimanikya Satapathy**, Founder & Editor-in-Chief, approved this batch with “go for it”. Prepared with AI assistance, continuing the [population atlas](language-population-atlas.md) and [language heritage model](language-heritage-model.md).

## What readers receive

- Thirty district profiles and a district directory, generated from the existing Census 2011 dataset. These are historical population profiles, not thirty newly field-researched destination stories.
- Each profile shows mutually exclusive language-group totals, their individual mother-tongue entries, and separate rural/urban breakdowns. Positive recorded entries are listed; no modern absence is inferred from an omitted row. Parent and child tables cannot be added together.
- Links connect population entries to the ten available language introductions. Existing destination guides for Bhubaneswar, Puri, Cuttack, Balasore and Mayurbhanj link to the relevant district profile. City and district geography are explicitly distinguished.
- Sambalpuri and Desia introductions include source-attributed creative and educational context, Census entry mappings and portable reading notes. Full literary histories and community naming review remain unfinished.
- A multilingual Odisha explainer derives a non-overlapping three-part distribution from C-17: no additional language reported, one additional language reported, two additional languages reported. The last category is included within “at least one additional language”. Counts are statewide; there is no district-level allocation or present-day fluency inference.

## Canonical data and interpretation

[Population data](../research/voices/population-2011.json) remain reproducibly extracted from unchanged workbooks. [Destination connections](../research/voices/district-connections.json) preserve editorial city/district scope. [Language story collection](../research/voices/collection.json) holds paragraph source IDs, editorial invitations, learning links and reading notes.

Census naming is retained in routes and headings: for example, the Balasore destination guide explicitly links to Baleshwar in the source. All district slugs are generated from unique source names. No geographic boundary map or inference about ethnicity, migration, ancestry or the language of a particular host is introduced.

Source additions answer missing creative/educational questions rather than repeat the population research. Sambalpuri uses a 2022 published interview with Mitrabhanu Gountia and Sambalpur district’s culture account. Desia uses a dated Ministry of Education workshop report and SCSTRTI’s January–June 2024 newsletter. The workshop report describes preparation and planned revision, not a completed contemporary rollout. Newsletter performances are historical events, not bookable activities. Catalogue labels such as Bhatri-Desia are not merged into the Census Desia entry.
