# Utkal Blueprint — governance and decision gates

**UTB-GOV-005 · v0.1 · 27 September 2026 · Governance wording approved by Founder**

The Founder approved this shared governance wording with “clean - next” after receiving draft v0.1. See the approval record. It follows the approved [Blueprint charter](charter.md). Project records name actual people, assignments and local arrangements. Existing UTP governance and hierarchy drafts remain project-specific proposals; this document does not approve them or the full operating-rule package.

## 1. Three areas of human authority

| Area | Governing human | Decisions covered |
|---|---|---|
| Blueprint stewardship | Ahimanikya; any future delegation recorded explicitly | Shared baseline, versions, permitted variations and Utkal provider commitments |
| Internal Utkal projects | Ahimanikya holds the final gate; future human leads act only within recorded delegation | Project charter, priorities, team, resources, publication and lifecycle |
| Independent adopting organizations | Their own authorized human leadership | Their local adoption, personnel, resources, delivery and participation |

Central coordination does not create a managerial reporting line. Cross-organization work requires authorization from each affected authority for its own scope. A project cannot promise another project’s resources merely because both use the Blueprint.

## 2. Reporting and assignments

Maintain four distinct relationships: organizational placement, human reporting, AI supervision, and collaboration. A relationship in one category does not grant the rights of another.

Each human assignment has one named primary human manager, except the top accountable human, who has no invented superior. Human reporting must have no cycles and no AI anywhere in the chain. Each AI assignment has a named human supervisor, purpose, permitted tools/data and limits. All future appointments remain vacant until a real human decision fills them.

Initially Ahimanikya holds the human accountability. The approved persona names—Disha, Anvesha, Samanta and Drishti—do not establish active assignments. Disha’s coordinator remit and all detailed JDs will be reviewed separately.

An AI coordinator may organize authorized work, collect updates, track dependencies and bring decisions to the responsible human. It cannot set a human’s commitments or exercise managerial authority.

## 3. Decision rights

| Decision | AI contribution | Required authority |
|---|---|---|
| Routine work within an approved brief | Research, draft, implement and check | Existing authorization; no repeated permission for necessary steps within scope |
| Charter, governance, delegation or project closure | Prepare options and consequences | Responsible human; Founder’s final gate for internal Utkal |
| Joining, moving, leaving or access changes | Prepare JML record and handover | Authorized human decision; access must match the actual assignment |
| Spending, membership fees or external commitments | Prepare exact terms and costs | Human approval covering the commitment; other parties accept separately |
| Publication, merge, deployment or baseline adoption | Prepare the exact candidate and evidence | Human authorization covering the revision, destination and action |
| Quality or evidence assessment | Report findings, uncertainty and limitations | Findings remain truthful; the human decides the action or documented risk acceptance |
| Shared version release and project upgrade | Prepare differences, impact and recovery | Separate release and adopter decisions, which may be approved together explicitly |

Titles, credentials, an AI recommendation and a passing check do not grant decision authority.

## 4. A proportionate path through the gates

1. **Define the work:** record the need, project, accountable human, deliverable, boundaries and acceptance evidence.
2. **Confirm authorization:** link the existing instruction or decision. Obtain any missing authority for the affected action.
3. **Prepare and check:** produce the concrete result, review it and disclose unresolved issues. Use evidence appropriate to the work—editorial checks, programme readiness or software validation as applicable.
4. **Take the human decision:** present the candidate, proposed action and consequences where approval is required. A bounded batch may receive one approval.
5. **Execute and verify:** perform authorized actions and record what actually happened. Approved, applied and verified are separate states.

Small, reversible work may use one short record. The gates do not require a meeting or a new permission request at every step. Existing authorization continues to cover work within its scope. Material changes to scope, cost, access or external effects require a new decision; unaffected authorized work may continue.

## 5. Decision records and escalation

For a decision, record its stable ID, actual human response and context, artifact revision, approved action and scope, conditions and date. An AI may transcribe this record but cannot supply a missing human approval. Preserve the reviewed candidate so later changes are distinguishable.

When authority is unclear, pause the affected action and explain the specific missing decision with a concrete proposal. When a finding conflicts with a desired outcome, retain the finding and record the human’s response separately. Internal conflicts and reserved decisions go to the Founder; external organizations use their own human escalation route.

## 6. Adoption and review scope

Each adopter records its Blueprint version, governing human, local charter, permitted variations and actual adoption decision. Updates are explicit. Local arrangements cannot waive human-only reporting or replace human authority with AI.
