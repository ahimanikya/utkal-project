# Utkal Blueprint — founding charter

**UTB-CHR-001 · v0.1 · 27 September 2026 · Charter wording approved by Founder**

**Utkal Blueprint — created by Ahimanikya Satapathy, Founder & Editor-in-Chief of Utkal Project.**

The Founder approved this charter’s wording with “Sounds good” after receiving draft v0.1 in this conversation. Its underlying directions—human authority, independent project governance, shared baseline, JML and adoption order—were already recorded. Approval covers this charter; linked rules, roles and implementation retain their separate review states. See the approval record.

## 1. Purpose

Utkal Blueprint helps organizations and teams work effectively with humans and AI together. It makes purpose, responsibility, authority, work and decisions explicit, while keeping humans in control.

The Blueprint begins with Utkal Project and will develop through practical use. It is also intended to become an offering for member organizations, supported by training and, where separately agreed, operating support.

## 2. What the Blueprint owns

The common operating model, rules and working baseline are defined at Blueprint level. They include governance, organization and reporting structures, human and AI member types, role and job-description templates, the working agreement, Joiner–Mover–Leaver (JML), work and decision records, review gates, and the process for changing and adopting the model.

Projects adopt a named Blueprint version and complete their own records within it.

| Shared Blueprint baseline | Defined by each adopting project or organization |
|---|---|
| Charter and governance structure | Purpose, audience, outcomes, scope and human decision holders |
| Role, JD and reporting templates | Actual members, assignments, responsibilities and reporting lines |
| Working agreement and operating rules | Applicable local arrangements within the adopted baseline |
| JML process and required records | Authorized membership changes and actual access |
| Work, review and decision model | Plans, priorities, deliverables and recorded decisions |
| Version and adoption process | Adopted version, permitted variations and upgrade decisions |

The shared structure supports independent charters, teams, working arrangements and lifecycles. Adoption does not create legal ownership, employment, a commercial commitment or access to another organization’s resources.

## 3. Human authority

Ahimanikya is the Founder & Editor-in-Chief, the sole initial human member, and the final authority for Utkal Blueprint and internal Utkal governance. Any later delegation must be an explicit human decision recording its holder, scope, limits and duration; the Founder retains the final internal gate.

Independent external adopters remain governed by their own authorized human leadership. Blueprint stewardship does not give Utkal authority over their personnel or operations. Any central support has a separately agreed scope and named human authorities on both sides.

**Humans report only to humans.** This applies to direct, indirect, functional, temporary and delegated reporting. No project variation may waive it.

## 4. AI participation

Human members and AI personas must be explicitly distinguished. Identity, assigned role, supervision and permissions are separate records. Every AI assignment has a named human supervisor and a bounded purpose.

AI may research, propose, draft, coordinate, implement and verify within actual authorization. AI cannot become a human’s manager, appoint or dismiss humans, create its own authority, approve its own recommendation, or replace a required human decision. An AI coordinator supports planning and follow-through; its title does not confer managerial authority.

Persona names, historical inspirations and job descriptions describe intended behavior. They do not activate an agent, grant access or constitute an appointment.

## 5. How work is governed

Work should have a clear purpose, an accountable human and enough recorded context for another person to understand its status. Proposals, approvals, implementation and verification must remain distinguishable. Approval applies to the concrete scope reviewed; silence or a general expression of support is not approval of unseen details.

JML governs joining, changing assignments and leaving for both humans and AI personas, with appropriate differences in supervision, access and handover. A template entry does not itself authorize a membership event.

Detailed gates, working rules and JML checklists will be reviewed separately. Their burden should be proportionate to the work and its consequences.

## 6. Changes and adoption

Blueprint changes are proposed, reviewed by the authorized human, and released as identifiable versions with a record of what changed. Each adopter explicitly approves its adoption or upgrade. Publishing a template update does not silently change an adopter’s governance, team or permissions.

Local variations must be recorded and remain within the baseline’s allowed scope. They cannot override human authority or permit humans to report to AI.

Central coordination uses only agreed records and access. Adopting the same Blueprint does not automatically share private information, credentials or AI memory. Managed support and exit arrangements require their own agreed terms.

## 7. Development and outcomes

Building the Blueprint and building Utkal Project are connected but distinct workstreams. The first adoption sequence is **Utkal Project → Utkal Collective → Utkal Unbound**. Bhubaneswar Alpha Lab and Bhubaneswar Athletic Club begin later.

The initial objective is a usable, reviewed baseline tested through bounded work in Utkal Project. Proposed measures of usefulness are clearer responsibility, traceable decisions, reliable handovers and less avoidable rework. Training and wider adoption should reflect observed experience; no effectiveness claims or numerical targets are established by this charter.
