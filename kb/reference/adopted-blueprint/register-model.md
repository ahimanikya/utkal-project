# Utkal registers and ledgers · 1.0.0

Created by Ahimanikya Satapathy, Founder & Editor-in-Chief of Utkal Project. Approved for UTB and UTP in the founding conversation; implementation prepared with AI assistance.

This is a versioned extension to Blueprint 0.1.0-rc.1. It implements the existing authoritative-work-record rule. It does not replace the previously adopted baseline, appoint members, change human authority or adopt the extension for UTC, UTU, BAL or BAC.

## Record once, derive the views

`registers/records.json` is the current structured register. `registers/activity.jsonl` is the append-only activity and handover ledger. `registers/DASHBOARD.md` is generated from both. Edit the source records and regenerate the dashboard; do not maintain a second status list in GitHub Issues or another service. A future integration needs one explicit authoritative source and stable ID mapping.

| Record collection | Purpose | Maintenance |
|---|---|---|
| projects | Project identity, human accountability, charter and adoption references | Each project owns its entry; central summaries link to adopter records |
| work | Tasks, issues, research gaps, corrections and requests distinguished by category | Owner, authorization, status, next action, blockers, readiness and evidence |
| decisions | Actual human direction and proposed decisions distinguished by status | Preserve exact supplied quotes, scope, actor and superseding references |
| requirements | PRDs and briefs linked to work | Index the authoritative document; do not duplicate its requirements |
| reviews | Findings, checks, limitations and independence | Passing review never grants release authority |
| releases | Actual publication/application events | Destination, evidence and the applicable human authorization |
| sources | Sources, assets, creator credit, rights and inspection depth | Index existing evidence; no automatic right to publish |
| relationships | Reported discussions, interest, agreements and commitments | Distinguish reported interest from an appointment or partnership |
| record_homes | Members, assignments, JML and evolution-history locations | Link to their existing canonical records; do not create competing rosters |
| optional_extensions | Contributions and heritage connections | Defined now; activate when their actual workflows begin |

The same model works for engineering, editorial, training and hospitality work. Projects can add domain fields and checks. Each adopter names its accountable human, actual governance and record locations. No human reports to AI; persona identity is distinct from membership, assignment and authority.

## IDs and relationships

Keep the agreed PROJECT-TYPE-NNN convention and all existing IDs; numbers may grow beyond three digits and are never reused. This extension uses WORK, DEC, REQ, REV, REL, REG, SRC (source or asset), EVT (activity event) and HIS (evolution history). The existing UTP-HIS four-digit series stays unchanged. Relationship records use REG IDs with category `relationship`. Version numbers are separate from identity. `refs` link records by ID; `evidence` links files or source URLs. External references must be explicitly declared with a location.

A current register row can be updated; its meaningful changes get an activity event. Earlier event lines and historical evidence are preserved. Corrections reference the record they amend. Necessary privacy redactions retain a dated explanation without reproducing the removed information.

## States and the human gate

Work states: proposed, ready, in_progress, blocked, awaiting_review, completed, deferred, cancelled. Readiness: concept, draft, reviewed, approved, applied, published. Track both: completing a research draft is not publishing it. A published item must link to a release record; a release must identify a human approval and publication evidence. Rejected or superseded decisions remain visible. Human approval is transcribed from the decision home, not generated by a validator.

Decision status: proposed, approved, rejected, superseded, recorded_direction. An approved decision has a human actor, exact supplied instruction or durable approval evidence, and bounded scope. Historical record dates are capture dates unless the actual event date is known.

Review outcomes: pass, pass_with_limitations, fail, not_tested. A self-review is identified as such. Keep failures and limitations even if the human accepts them. Automated validation checks record consistency, not the truth of historical claims or the authenticity of human approval.

## Working practice

1. Read the dashboard, linked brief and latest activity before substantive work.
2. Reuse an existing work item or create one with its actual authorization and human accountability. Do not assign work to a persona beyond its approved scope.
3. Update material progress, decisions, blockers, evidence and next actions in the source register; append activity events.
4. At handover, record where the artifacts are, what remains and the next step. An interrupted session is not completion.
5. Record actual reviews and the human's decisions separately. Publication requires authorization covering that action and candidate.
6. Run `python3 tools/registers.py` to validate and regenerate. Use `--check` to detect stale views. No server, paid service, automatic agent or scheduled task is required.
7. Preserve significant reasons and milestones in the evolution ledger. It explains the journey; it does not replace operational records.

## Small starting footprint

Begin with one JSON register, one activity ledger and one generated dashboard per project. The dashboard includes work, pending decisions, readiness, review and release history, source/asset inventory and relationships. Team/JML records and narrative history stay in their established homes. Contribution and heritage extensions remain inactive until commissioned. No GitHub Issues or Projects board is created by this implementation.

The Blueprint supplies structure and a portable validator/generator. Each project retains independent decisions, lifecycle and access boundaries. Fleet views may summarize approved metadata; they cannot grant authority or copy restricted material into broader visibility.
