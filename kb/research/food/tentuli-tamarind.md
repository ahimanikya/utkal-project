# Tentuli: fruit, seed and the collector economy

**Tentuli is tamarind, *Tamarindus indica*.** The pulp used in food, the hard seed, seeded sale lots and de-seeded pulp need separate records.

## Season and food uses

The Balasore field survey conducted in **January 2018–February 2020** records **January–April** fruiting for tentuli. It describes pulp in pickles, chutney and curry, and tender leaves as a vegetable. These are reported uses, not fully specified recipes or present market availability. [Table 1, row 72, printed p.455](https://www.botanyjournals.com/assets/archives/2020/vol5issue3/5-3-106-999.pdf).

SCSTRTI's historical community study lists **Titel / Tentuli** among products marketed in its **Lanjia Saora, Jantar** village account (Table 2.16, printed p.50). This adds community and place attribution without establishing quantities or earnings. Its seasonal tables also mention occasional tamarind-seed gruel, but near-identical tables across communities and missing cooking endpoints make this a lead for local review, not a publishable recipe. The report's fieldwork/publication dates remain unresolved; 2014 occurs in the repository filename. [Study](https://repository.tribal.gov.in/bitstream/123456789/74427/1/SCST_2014_research_0338.pdf).

## Follow the collector's sale

Vasundhara's *MSP Denied* records **Sitarani Hasda of the Rairangpur region** reporting a sale of **two quintals for ₹5,000**. This is a historical self-reported transaction; the interview date and seeded/de-seeded grade are unclear. It is not an Odisha price average, net profit or a current quote. The same booklet includes collectors' accounts of difficulty accessing procurement. [Ground testimonies, PDF p.11](https://vasundharaodisha.org/upload/pdfFile/b949225825e4667de86b4628a67b57b8.pdf).

The booklet's policy rates and statewide production claims require original dated records. TDCCOL's tender could not be retrieved through the web or direct download; no tender quantity, achieved procurement or realised sale is adopted. This matters for the eventual business story: a support price, collector receipt and finished-product retail price describe different stages.

## Forest origin and cultivation

The Balasore source classifies its entries as wild edible plants; this does not certify the origin of market lots. ICAR-CIAH lists a tamarind cultivar, **Goma Prateek**, showing a separate horticultural research pathway. Odisha planting, named local cultivars and the split between forest, community and privately grown supply remain unverified.
