# Mahua and mohula: flowers, seeds and changing uses

**Mahua is more than a beverage story.** Record the tree, its flowers and its seed products separately, then connect them through attributed household knowledge and trade evidence.

## Names and identity

IISc's Herbarium JCB records *Madhuca longifolia* var. *latifolia*, with *Madhuca indica* and *Madhuca latifolia* among its synonyms. Its Odia-name field includes Mahula and Mahua and lists occurrence in Odisha districts. This supplies a botanical reference, not a census of productive trees. [IISc flora](https://indiaflora-ces.iisc.ac.in/FloraPeninsular/herbsheet.php?cat=7&id=8868).

The user's spelling **Mohua** is retained as a search alias. Mohula/mahula (ମହୁଲ, editorial script pending language review) belongs here; **Mohu (ମହୁ)** means honey in the user's clarification and has a [separate profile](mohu-honey.md).

## Odisha food-processing research · 2016 issue

Bakhara, Bal, Pal, Sahoo and Panda reported surveying mahua post-harvest practices in Odisha and developing foods including candied and glazed flowers, a bar, drinks, squash, jam, laddu and cake. Their affiliations include OUAT's agricultural processing and food-engineering department. The paper appeared in *Agricultural Engineering Today*40(4), pp.22–28; the publisher dates its online release to24 February2017. [Publisher abstract](https://indianjournals.com/article/aet-40-4-004).

This supports a research-and-food-diversity story. It does not establish that these formulations are ancestral recipes, widely sold products or profitable businesses. The abstract supplies no usable recipe quantities or consumer-panel methods; those remain full-text questions. ## A documented sal-leaf cake and local harvest season

Behera and colleagues' 2016 paper reports 2009 fieldwork among Santhal residents around Chandbill, Bangiriposi, Mayurbhanj. Its collection account places the flower season in **March–April** and includes **private as well as forest land**. This is a local historical account, not a present statewide calendar.

Under “Cake” (printed p.98), it describes **250 g mahua flowers and 500 g rice**, separately soaked for **one hour**, then ground together. The paste is enclosed between sal leaves and fire-cooked. The paper mentions alternative starch ingredients but gives no cooking time or temperature. This is an attributed preparation description, not a kitchen-tested recipe; flower moisture state and practical cooking endpoints remain unclear. [Original paper](https://atr.vsu.edu.ph/article/download/212/202/390).

Its marketing discussion describes sales to local intermediaries under storage and cash constraints. It supplies no usable collector-price series here. Do not translate that qualitative account into present earnings or assume identical conditions across communities.

## Later university research

OUAT's 2020–21 report records development of a **10 kg-per-batch unit** for mahua candy and nectar/syrup, and a cookie experiment using mahua powder. These are research outputs, not verified commercial production or deployed businesses. [Annual report, printed p.23](https://ouat.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/annual_report_2020-21_3.pdf).

The earlier Agricultural Engineering Today abstract and this field paper are different publications. Their food accounts must not be collapsed into one study or one recipe tradition.
