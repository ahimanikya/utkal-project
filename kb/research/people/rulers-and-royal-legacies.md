# Utkal’s rulers and royal legacies

Utkal’s royal history can be explored through surviving places, inscriptions and institutions. Start with five rulers whose documented legacies connect ancient Kalinga, the Eastern Gangas and the Suryavamsi Gajapatis.

| Ruler | Historical setting | Why include them? | Evidence route |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Kharavela](kharavela.md) | Ancient Kalinga; exact chronology disputed | Royal record of campaigns, welfare and Jain connections | Hathigumpha inscription and cave heritage |
| [Anantavarman Chodaganga Deva](anantavarman-chodaganga.md) | Eastern Ganga; twelfth-century temple association | Major royal patronage at Puri | Government attribution of Jagannath Temple |
| [Anangabhima Deva III](anangabhima-deva-iii.md) | Eastern Ganga; early thirteenth century | Cuttack as a centre of government | Historical discussion of Nagari plates and charters |
| [Narasimha Deva I](narasimha-deva-i.md) | Eastern Ganga; 1238–1264 in UNESCO account | Konark and its architectural legacy | UNESCO’s site history |
| [Kapilendra Deva](kapilendra-deva.md) | Suryavamsi Gajapati; fifteenth century | An inscriptional presence beyond today’s Odisha | Srisailam record dated 1460 by its editor |

**Power, contribution and remembrance are three different questions.** Inscriptions can reveal campaigns and royal claims; buildings and grants can document patronage; later traditions can show how a ruler was remembered. None of these alone measures popularity among all subjects.

[Konark Sun Temple](../places/konark.md) connects royal-period history with a surviving World Heritage monument. [Jagannath Temple, Puri](../places/jagannath-temple-puri.md) connects patronage with an existing religious-place record. These reuse the encyclopedia’s place pages rather than creating duplicate monuments.

Ancient Kalinga and historical Utkal had changing meanings and boundaries. This collection is not a claim that all these kingdoms had the borders of modern Odisha. No empire-area, revenue or trade-volume estimate has been created from royal titles.
