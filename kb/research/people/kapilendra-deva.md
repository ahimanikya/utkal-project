# Kapilendra Deva (Kapilesvara)

**Kapilendra Deva’s story extends beyond today’s Odisha through the inscriptional record.** A Srisailam slab discussed by D. C. Sircar in Epigraphia Indica names Gajapati Kapilesvara of Orissa. Its Odia and Telugu-character sections provide a concrete connection to southern India. Sircar dates the record to **Saka 1382 / 1460 CE**.

Bharati Pal’s government-published historical article uses Kapilendra and Kapilesvara for the founding Suryavamsi Gajapati ruler. The names are retained as aliases for this profile, with the original inscriptional spelling visible.

The epigraphic edition is unusually useful because it also states the limits of the evidence: the editor calls an expansive territorial epithet hyperbolic, and does not establish whether the king personally visited Srisailam or which donation the damaged record originally described. The inscription supports a southern epigraphic connection; it cannot by itself establish permanent control of every territory named.

See the [edition](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/50721.pdf), article 10, printed pp. 75–78. Future work should link independently dated Warangal and other records, preserving conquest, overlordship, administration and royal rhetoric as separate claims.
