# Buddhist learning in Odisha: Pushpagiri and the monastic landscape

**Pushpagiri is a promising research focus for the ancient learning question.** The [Jajpur exhibition](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/the-buddhist-heritage-of-jajpur-jajpur/NwWh9wDVsCTFtg?hl=en), whose story is credited to Live History India, identifies Langudi with Pushpagiri and connects it to Xuanzang’s account. This is a curated interpretation, not our own reading of an inscription or excavation report.

We already maintain [Ratnagiri](../places/ratnagiri.md), [Lalitgiri](../places/lalitgiri.md) and [Udayagiri](../places/udayagiri-buddhist.md). Keep them as distinct archaeological identities. A modern circuit name does not prove they formed one university.

The primary-source checkpoint below now distinguishes the traveller account, excavated identities and competing Langudi interpretations. No institutional ranking, enrolment or uninterrupted modern university lineage is established.

A broader Buddhist history should distinguish Ashokan inscriptions and the Kalinga-war account, later monastic phases, Buddhist philosophical traditions, objects and manuscripts, and living communities. Links to Southeast Asia require evidence for each person, object or text, rather than an assumed universal influence.

## A landscape of learning, with names preserved in clay

The strongest story combines a traveller’s words with named archaeological evidence. Li Rongxi’s translation of Xuanzang describes the people of Uḍa as devoted to learning and then names Pushpagiri monastery. Separately, the ASI excavation report identifies **Simhaprastha Mahavihara at Udayagiri-2** through seals and sealings. These are different evidence types, connected by region rather than assumed institutional identity.

### What the traveller’s numbers mean

The text describes **more than 100 monasteries and over 10,000 monks across Uḍa**, before introducing Pushpagiri. The numbers belong to the wider historical country. They do not describe Pushpagiri enrolment and are not a modern Odisha census. Miracles described at the stupas remain religious testimony. [Li Rongxi, 1996, Fascicle X, pp.269–270](https://bdkamerica.org/download/1865).

### Why the Langudi identification stays attributed

The report quotes B.N. Mukherjee’s readings and D.R. Pradhan’s identification arguments. It also records Bandyopadhyay’s reservations after a 1999 visit and calls for further examination. The original Langudi inscription articles and plates have not yet been inspected in this project. The2007 disagreement is a dated scholarly record; it does not prove that no later work exists. Do not promote an Ashokan foundation or a contested inscription date to established fact.
