# Scientific knowledge preserved in manuscripts

Odisha’s manuscript heritage gives the science timeline a second route: follow surviving catalogue records alongside biographies. The museum’s undated department page describes approximately 37,000 manuscripts across 27 categories, including mathematics, astronomy/astrology and medicine. Its separate collection table gives 37,273. Neither is a current science-manuscript count or a measure of unique works. The pages provide no common inventory date, so no change is calculated. [Department](https://odishamuseum.nic.in/?q=dept/manuscripts); [collection table](https://www.odishamuseum.nic.in/?q=node/5).

## Bhāsvatī beyond Odisha

IGNCA’s 2024 catalogue, printed p.102, records *Bhāsvatī* witnesses at BORI in Pune, Kamarupa Anusandhan Samiti in Guwahati and Saraswati Bhawan Library in Varanasi. Four selected entries retain catalogue identifiers, folio counts, completeness and scripts in the [evidence register](../references/data/science-heritage.json). The Guwahati entry credits a commentary to Karirāja Cakravarti. These are catalogue descriptions of manuscripts represented in IGNCA’s microfilm collection, not four independent historical studies. [Catalogue](https://ignca.gov.in/PDF_data/Jyotish-Vol-6-Part-1.pdf#page=120).

This extends the [Bhāsvatī work record](../works/bhasvati.md) with institutional catalogue evidence. Repository locations demonstrate documented preservation beyond Odisha; they do not establish when the text travelled, its influence abroad or a continuous scientific institution. The work’s composition date and a surviving copy’s date are different fields. Unspecified Saṁvat dates remain unconverted.

## Mathematics on palm leaf

The museum’s [Ganita catalogue](https://odishamuseum.nic.in/?field_abstraction_tid=All&field_author_value=&field_category_tid=All&field_subject_value=&page=1&q=palm-leaf-g) lists *Lilabati Pati Ganita* (G/141), *Ganita Chautisha* (G/43) and *Khadi Pothi* (G/112) as palm-leaf records. Their displayed author fields are blank. Names, dates, language interpretation and intellectual content need manuscript-level checks before these can become credited scientist profiles. A title containing a poetic form offers a research lead for science and literary expression, not proof of a particular teaching practice.

## What remains open

INSA’s bibliography confirms Deepak Bhattacharya’s eight-page 2008 survey in *Indian Journal of History of Science* 43(1), pp.101–108. The full survey could not be retrieved; its table entries remain unverified leads. [Publisher index](https://insaindia.res.in/history-of-science/).

The Devīdāsa commentary lead in the already saved Panda study still needs an identifiable manuscript record. No new early scientist has been inferred from surname, text title or custody alone. Original folios, colophons, acquisition histories and specialist attribution remain the next evidence layer. Museum scanning and online catalogue presence do not establish complete digitisation, public full-text access or reuse rights.
