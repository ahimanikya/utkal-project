# Jagannath: sacred narratives, inscriptions and the making of Puri

Puri’s story can be read through sacred narratives, royal patronage and the material support of worship. A **1225 inscription records land given to support food offerings**. It is a concrete point where devotion, farming and food meet. It does not date the beginning of Jagannath worship or measure today’s temple economy.

## The sacred narrative

In the Purusottama-ksetra-mahatmya translated by G. V. Tagare, Indradyumna is a king at Avanti. He sends Vidyapati to seek the deity; Vidyapati encounters the Sabara devotee Visvavasu. Visvavasu guides him to Nilamadhava and the narrative anticipates Indradyumna’s installation of wooden divine forms. Vidyapati returns to the king with a garland from the deity. These are episodes in this textual telling, with a cosmological setting, not dated biographical events. [Ch.7, vv.4–6,12–14,48–50,85–99](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc370727.html); [ch.8, vv.1–18](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc370728.html); [ch.9, vv.20–34](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc370729.html).

The text’s forest setting and Sabara participants deserve their own account. They do not by themselves establish the date, route or social process by which worship developed. The district’s [Kantilo Nilamadhab account](https://nayagarh.odisha.gov.in/en/tourism/tourist-places/kantilo-nilamadhab) preserves another place association. Utkal has not established a dated transfer from Kantilo to Puri. Other tellings, including Lalita and the mustard-seed episode, need their own named textual sources before being combined with this version.

The epigraphic anchor is D. C. Sircar’s edition of **four inscriptions in the Patalesvara shrine within the Jagannath complex**. He describes their language as old Odia with some Sanskrit. This is also a route into Odisha’s language history. [Epigraphia Indica XXX, article 34, printed pp.197–200; PDF pp.274–277](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/35546.pdf).

Sircar distinguishes the Madala Panji construction tradition from Ganga family records crediting Chodaganga, and reports the scholarly interpretation that Anangabhima completed work begun by Chodaganga. Record 2 names Halin, Chakrin and Subhadra, while its final sentence remains difficult to interpret. A dated inscription in the complex is not itself a foundation certificate. His suggestion about the Patalesvara shrine’s earlier use is a question, not an established earlier sanctum. [Edition, pp.197,199](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/35546.pdf).
