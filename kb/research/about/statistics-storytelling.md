# Making Odisha’s numbers easy to understand

The user wants statistics to be consumable, meaningful and easy to read. Lead with a memorable takeaway, a clearly labelled number and its relevance to the reader. Keep the research depth available behind the experience.

## The public unit: one small story

1. **Headline:** say what changed or why the detail is interesting.
2. **Hero number:** one main statistic, with its measure and period alongside it.
3. **Meaning:** one sentence explaining what it tells us. Connect it to a person, place or experience only where evidence supports that connection.
4. **Visual:** use the simplest form that makes the point clear: a labelled comparison, a short trend, a map or a credited photograph.
5. **Invitation and evidence:** offer a relevant next step and an expandable source/method panel. Keep any qualification that changes the headline’s meaning visible beside the number.

Aim for a takeaway in three seconds, understanding in thirty seconds, and deeper evidence on demand. These are design targets, not measured audience results. Test actual comprehension with readers later.

## Make the format fit the subject

- **Growth:** one headline and a small chart showing the full relevant period. Label the start and end; show intervening years when available.
- **Food:** a named dish, an appetising credited photograph, its place association and a short reason to try it. Add a serving venue once established.
- **Heritage:** a distinctive detail, a strong image and a clear location. Add visitor information where checked.
- **Facilities:** readable service labels with location and freshness information. Distinguish “listed by the operator” from currently confirmed availability.
- **Regional collections:** a map or a few destination cards with filters. A research entry count is a count of this collection, not Odisha’s total food or heritage inventory.

Default to one main number per card, one story per poster and one main idea per short video. On an opening screen, use at most three supporting facts before inviting the reader to explore. These are project defaults; clarity takes precedence over a rigid count.

### Tourism: a recovery story

**Tourist visits rose 13.1% in 2024**

**1.11 crore visits** · Odisha · calendar year 2024

Up from 97.7 lakh in 2023—about 12.8 lakh additional recorded visits in a year.

**Visible context:** This is a one-year recovery comparison. Visits remained below the 2019 level of 1.54 crore. Counts cover domestic and foreign tourist visits, not unique people.

**Visual direction:** show 2019, 2023 and 2024 as three labelled bars starting at zero. Identify them as selected years; do not draw a continuous annual trend through missing years.

**Next action:** Explore Odisha’s destinations.

**Evidence:** [2023–2024 comparison](../economy/comparison-10-tourism.md) and [2019 benchmark](../economy/comparison-09-tourism.md). For an international edition, display 11.1 million visits instead of 1.11 crore. Exact values remain in the linked records.

### Manufacturing: growth with its basis explained

**Manufacturing’s economic contribution grew 26% over five years**

**26% real growth** · Odisha · FY2019–20 to FY2024–25

The sector added more economic value after adjusting for price changes. This measure does not establish growth in jobs, company sales or investment.

**Visible caption:** Manufacturing gross state value added at constant 2011–12 prices. FY2024–25 is a first revised estimate. Rounded from 26.2%.

**Visual direction:** show all six saved annual observations so the rise, subsequent fall and recovery remain visible. Avoid an arrow implying uninterrupted annual growth.

## Readable numbers and honest visuals

Use lakh and crore for Indian audiences; use million and billion for international audiences. Keep the currency visible and avoid currency conversion unless its date and exchange-rate basis are saved. Round deliberately: one decimal place is usually enough for percentages. Calculate changes from the underlying values before rounding.

Keep calendar years separate from financial years; real growth separate from nominal growth; percentage change separate from percentage-point change; visitors separate from visits; announcements separate from realised outcomes. Rankings need the ranked group, measure and year beside them.

Use direct labels, readable text and strong contrast. Avoid 3D charts, decorative gauges, crowded legends and colour-only meanings. Bar charts begin at zero. Explain a restricted line-chart scale when used. Do not hide a material benchmark or estimate status in a source drawer.

Sources, exact values, calculation notes and fuller tables belong in the evidence view, with downloadable data available for readers who want it. Public pages should answer a reader’s question before presenting additional detail.

## Keep curiosity connected to action

A tourism statistic can introduce an exploration page. A food story can lead to a confirmed maker or venue. A textile story can lead to a credited artisan and purchase route. A production statistic can lead to a sourced industry profile. Do not infer job creation, tourist spending or local income from a different measure.

Measure whether readers understand and remember the takeaway, then whether they save, share, explore or enquire. Positive audience response is a research question until observed.
