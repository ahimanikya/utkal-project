# Sabitri Brata

Savitri’s conversation with Yama gives this observance a literary history that can be read directly.

**Narrative research checkpoint**. Traditional month: Jyestha. No annual visit date is confirmed.

## A woman follows and argues

In the Mahabharata passage preserved in K. M. Ganguli’s translation, Satyavan collapses while collecting wood. When Yama takes his life, Savitri follows. She speaks about duty, friendship and compassion; Yama repeatedly grants boons while withholding Satyavan’s life. Her request for children through Satyavan exposes the contradiction. He is restored. The episode gives Savitri a sustained speaking and reasoning role, not merely a moment of passive waiting.

Sources: [Mahabharata, Vana Parva: Savitri and Yama](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/m03/m03295.htm). Vana Parva, section displayed as CCLXLV, printed pp.576–581.

## Textual history is not a local origin date

An epic passage is direct evidence for the story. It does not tell us when a particular Odisha household began its annual brata or when its food customs took shape. That requires Odia katha editions, local histories and carefully attributed practice accounts. The pan-Indian story and the regional observance are connected research subjects, not interchangeable records.

Sources: [Mahabharata, Vana Parva: Savitri and Yama](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/m03/m03295.htm). Scope of the inspected epic passage.
