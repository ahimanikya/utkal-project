# Kanji Anla Osha

Rice-water preparations and a ritual offering invite a closer look at a less-documented part of the food year.

**Early research notes: history and local narrative remain incomplete**. Traditional month: Margasira. No annual visit date is confirmed.

## What the sources describe

Dash associates Kanji Anla with Sathi and lists kanji, radish and other materials. Mahanti describes kanji from fermented rice gruel and dried-fish offerings in an evening observance. The accounts should be compared locally; the study does not turn their lists into one standard recipe.

Sources: [The Perishing Tradition of Osha, Brata and Mela in Orissa: An Overview](https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/Orissareview/April2006/engpdf/The_perishing_tradition_of_osha.pdf), [Fading Festivals of Odisha: A peep into the religious practices in Odisha](https://www.esamskriti.com/essays/pdf/17%20FINAL%20Fading%20Festivals%20of%20Odisha%20%281%29.pdf). Dash pp.15,18 note 2; Mahanti p.60.

## A food-science question

There is a worthwhile distinction between recording a fermentation practice and measuring it. A future preparation record should identify ingredients, water, vessel, temperature, time and subsequent cooking. These variables are research questions; we have not tested this preparation, identified its organisms or demonstrated a probiotic benefit.

## The narrative remains open

A local katha, historical editions and the relationship to Sathi or Sasthi need further study. The use of dried fish also means that a blanket claim that all osha offerings are vegetarian would erase relevant evidence. This page retains the difference without claiming every household follows it.
