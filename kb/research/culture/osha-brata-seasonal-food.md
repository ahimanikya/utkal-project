# Osha, brata: stories, seasons and food

**Bara Masa, Tera Parba** is the invitation to this collection: read the year through stories, cooking, offering, restraint and sharing. Osha, brata and parba overlap in everyday speech; individual records retain the names and distinctions of their sources. Thirteen is not treated as a complete festival count.

## Stories to begin with

The eighteen selected profiles include seven narrative checkpoints and eleven early research notes. Nuakhai is included as an agrarian parba. The Chaitra Mangala story checkpoint uses an English synopsis; its full Odia text remains unread. The latter make their missing stories and histories explicit. None claims a securely established origin date.

## What the collection connects

Khudurukuni leads into maritime memory; Manabasa into grain, feeding and caste; Sabitri into a directly readable epic dialogue. Other records begin with a food sequence, an object or a source disagreement. These are different entry points, not claims of a single origin for Odisha’s observances.

Each page distinguishes a ritual narrative from documented history, attributes practice descriptions, and records open questions. Source recovery has promoted the earlier four indexed food leads to original-text descriptions. It has also exposed disagreements: Alana’s deity, Bada Osha’s date, Sudasa’s month and the processing terminology around khuda remain qualified.

## The research loop

Next, recover identified Odia katha editions for Sudasa, Budhei and Dutiya, record their imprint and passages, compare versions, then revise the same pages. Subsequent batches widen the geography, especially western Odisha, before extending the remaining name inventory. New names are research leads; one coastal account cannot establish statewide practice. The [structured study](../references/data/osha-culture.json) preserves the sequence and coverage status.

For food technology, record ingredients, utensils, leaf wrapping, fermentation, heat, timing and labour. A measured scientific finding must have its own evidence; a seasonal association does not prove a medical benefit or a historical theory.
