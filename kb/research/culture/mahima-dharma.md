## A faith expressed through poetry

Mahima Swami, also called Mahima Gosain, is described as the movement’s founder; Bhima Bhoi gave its ideas a widely remembered poetic voice. The existing literary introduction remains the person’s canonical narrative. This extension adds historical context and [credited reading routes](../works/stuti-chintamani.md).

### Alekha and the formless divine

Mahapatra’s monograph explains **Alekha** as indescribable and **nirakara** as formless, alongside other names for the divine. Its discussion of **sunya** belongs to a particular religious and literary interpretation. The use of a shared term does not make Mahima Dharma identical to Buddhist philosophy, nor establish one undisputed origin. [Mahapatra, printed pp. 17–20; PDF pp. 24–27](https://sahitya-akademi.gov.in/pdf/Bhima-Bhoi.pdf). These are descriptions of belief, not scientific claims.

### Different orders and remembered lives

Beltz discusses **Balkaladhari** and **Kaupinadhari** orders, their distinctive dress and differences over authority and Bhima Bhoi’s reception. This is evidence of diversity within the tradition; one account cannot stand for every adherent or today’s institutional arrangements. His chapter distinguishes hagiography—stories expressing religious meaning—from a reconstruction of verifiable life events. [Beltz, pp.230–238,244–245; note 29, p. 251](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10900/138250/Beltz_035.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1).
