# Mining-area funds: from allocations to working services

**9,690 projects were reported completed across six sampled Odisha DMFs by March 2024.** The same audit records 17,435 sanctioned projects. Completion is a useful milestone; staff, utilities, maintenance and actual use tell us whether a service works. [CAG Report 7 of 2026, printed pp.29–31](https://cag.gov.in/webroot/uploads/download_audit_report/2026/Report-No.-7-of-the-year-2026_PMKKKY-06aba7c29ed78b2.53467972.pdf).

This is a sample of Dhenkanal, Jajpur, Keonjhar, Mayurbhanj, Nabarangpur and Sundargarh—not a statewide project census. The audit covers 2015–16 to 2023–24, with later inspections and January 2026 government responses. None of these dates establishes October 2026 operation.

## Follow the money through separate stages

```mermaid
flowchart LR
 C[Collections including interest] --> S[Project sanctions]
 S --> R[Funds released]
 R --> E[Expenditure]
 E --> P[Reported completion]
 P --> U[Evidence of functioning service]
```

These are checkpoints to investigate, not proof that every funded project passed each one. In the six districts, collections including interest were ₹22,568.17 crore; sanctions ₹20,947.52 crore; releases ₹13,160.75 crore; expenditure ₹10,092.53 crore. Do not add the stages or equate these collections with government mining royalties. The existing statewide ₹26,602.66 crore figure covers leaseholder contributions to 30 DMFs and has a different scope. [Table 5.1, p.29](https://cag.gov.in/webroot/uploads/download_audit_report/2026/Report-No.-7-of-the-year-2026_PMKKKY-06aba7c29ed78b2.53467972.pdf).

Keep the unfinished work visible: 6,551 sampled projects were ongoing, and 1,194 were in the table’s not-started/dropped category. Detailed district rows belong in the [optional evidence view](../references/data/mining-dmf.json), rather than the main story.

## Four project cases, four separate evidence trails

| Project | What the audit establishes | Government response reproduced in the audit |
|---|---|---|
| Keonjhar DHH intensive-care units |₹2.18 crore spent; equipment installed March 2022. At 29 April 2025 inspection, the units were unused and staff were not deployed. |January 2026 response emphasised strengthened infrastructure; CAG said it did not address non-use. |
| Digital medical records in three Sundargarh hospitals |₹1.32 crore paid. The report gives different cessation dates for the hospitals; the 4 March 2025 DHH inspection found the system nonfunctional. |Government acknowledged software-compatibility problems and unsuccessful vendor engagement. |
| Deogan hostels, Sukinda |₹4.34 crore construction cost; CAG cites a 16 July 2022 completion certificate. The 10 December 2024 visit found no functioning accommodation, with utilities and handover pending. |Government said handover would follow critical utilities; CAG retained its finding. |
| Birsa Munda Hockey Stadium, Rourkela |Functional at the January 2025 audit visit. The ₹136.77 crore figure is the DMF-funded expenditure, not the whole stadium cost. |Government cited wider public interest and Sports Department permission; CAG disputed the eligibility of DMF financing. |

Sources: [sections 6.5.1 and 6.6.3–6.6.6, printed pp.46, 60–62](https://cag.gov.in/webroot/uploads/download_audit_report/2026/Report-No.-7-of-the-year-2026_PMKKKY-06aba7c29ed78b2.53467972.pdf). These are audit findings and recorded responses, not independent field visits by Utkal. A functioning asset can still have a funding dispute; a completed building can still lack a service. No current supplier, staffing or operating claims follow automatically.

## A source discrepancy stays visible

The p.29 prose prints 79.69% for expenditure relative to releases; its table prints 76.69%, which matches the displayed amounts. We retain both in the evidence record and withhold the inconsistent prose rate. A separate eight-hostel cost passage also needs component reconciliation; it is not the Deogan project.
