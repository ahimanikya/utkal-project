# Odisha’s rice economy: from paddy to value

**Rice is both a staple and a chain of livelihoods.** Start with production, then distinguish what farmers sell, what mills process and what consumers buy.

## The scale of the crop

Odisha Agriculture Statistics 2023–24 reports **115.39 lakh tonnes of rice**, across **40.87 lakh crop-hectares**, with average rice yield **2,823 kg/ha**. The same table reports **174.83 lakh tonnes of paddy**. These are two product bases for the crop, not quantities to add together. Seasonal crop area is not unique land or a farmer count. [Printed p.65](https://agri.odisha.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-05/OAS%20A4.pdf).

The existing [agriculture series](../statistics/agriculture.md) retains the separately sourced 2024–25 rice estimate of 118.6 lakh tonnes. No growth rate has been spliced across these publications in this edition.

## Procurement is one part of the market

The department’s 2024–25 report lists **70.89 lakh tonnes of paddy procured in KMS 2023–24**, including **12.36 lakh tonnes in Bargarh**, and **1,372 participating millers**. Millers participating in procurement are not a census of every rice business. [Annexure I, p.12; milling, p.27](https://www.foododisha.in/Download/annual-activity-glance-24-25.pdf).

For **KMS 2024–25**, that report gives common-paddy MSP of ₹2,300/quintal, Grade-A MSP of ₹2,320, and additional state assistance of ₹800/quintal. The common-paddy combination is ₹3,100/quintal for the applicable scheme: a historical policy rate, not a current retail quote or net profit. Registration, quality and scheme conditions matter. [Report p.9](https://www.foododisha.in/Download/annual-activity-glance-24-25.pdf).

## Where value can be measured next

| Part of the chain | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|
| Seed keeping and seed sale | Named varieties, growers, volumes, seed prices and benefit sharing |
| Farming | District/season yield, paid costs, family labour, land costs and actual sale receipts |
| Procurement | Marketing year, accepted quantity, farmers paid and payment timing |
| Milling | Paddy input, rice recovery, broken grain, bran and husk; processing and transport costs |
| Distinctive rice | Traceable origin, actual farmgate and retail transactions for the same product/date |
| Trade | Odisha-origin shipments, destination, quantity and realised value |

A retail premium is not the farmer’s premium. Farm receipts minus clearly defined costs are needed before claiming profitability. Production multiplied by MSP is not measured statewide revenue. Shipments through an Odisha port do not by themselves establish Odisha-grown rice exports.

## Yield and profit can move differently

Samant and colleagues report a coastal Odisha experiment in 2019–20 and 2020–21: transplanted rice had higher yield, but direct-seeded rice had higher reported net return. Transplanting’s mean cost was ₹77,816/ha and gross return ₹112,353/ha; direct seeding’s reported net return was ₹39,121/ha. [Original ORYZA abstract, published 30 June 2023](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/OIJR/article/view/138554).

This is a study-period experimental comparison. The full itemised cost basis and price schedule are still needed before interpreting it as an all-cost profit comparison, a representative household outcome or a current budget. It does not justify choosing a farming method without local agronomic advice.

## What remains after growing the rice?

A regional study published in March 2023 reports **₹79,050/ha gross income** and **₹14,141.58/ha net income after C2 costs** for its paddy case. C2 includes valued family labour, land and owned-capital costs. Its A2 cost of ₹41,013.10/ha also includes depreciation and owned inputs; it is not simply cash spending. [Sahoo and colleagues, Tables 5–7](https://ndpublisher.in/countpdfdownload.php?id=5788&pdf=EAv68n1p.pdf).

The full study sampled 120 farmers across six clusters; the paddy-specific sample and survey year are unclear. Context tables use 2018–19, which we have not assigned to the paddy case. These nominal historical values cannot be extrapolated to statewide farmer profit or compared with current MSP as a margin.
