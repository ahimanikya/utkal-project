# Sal: leaf craft, seeds and seasonal care

**Sal (*Shorea robusta*) is a tree with several distinct product streams.** Its leaves support plates, bowls and wrapping; its seeds belong to a separate oilseed chain. Keep it distinct from the siali climber and from edible forest-food ingredients.

The Balliguda working plan for **2021–22 to 2030–31** names **September–January** as an ideal leaf-collection window. It cautions against stripping plants and collecting tender foliage. This is a local management prescription, not an observed annual collection series or present statewide permission to harvest. Its botanical account places fruit maturity in **May–June**; fruiting, collection and sale are different events. [Printed pp.199–200](https://odishaforest.in/admin/data/documents/div_working_plan_file_831468672.pdf).

The [siali profile](siali-products.md) retains a named Nayagarh maker's historical account of sal hand stitching alongside machine-sewn siali. That testimony connects material knowledge and household work without treating every maker's practice as identical.

[Mahua preparation](../food/mahua-mohula.md) also records sal leaves as a cooking wrapper in a separate Santhal field account. A wrapper is not an edible ingredient; a seed-oil use description is not a home extraction or food-safety recipe.
