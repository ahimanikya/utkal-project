# Mining, land and people

**A mine is also a neighbour.** Its story follows water beyond a lease boundary, workers along a haul road, communities making decisions about land, and the life that returns after excavation. Production figures alone cannot answer those questions.

These are four bounded research studies, not current safety assurances or completed public stories. Each separates the place, evidence period, source role, reported finding and next record. [Structured cases](../references/data/mining-programme.json) retain the follow-up fields under `environment_rights_safety_checkpoint`.

## Sukurangi: read the sample before the conclusion

OMC's expansion summary describes a March–May 2022 baseline: air monitoring at ten locations and water sampling from three surface and six groundwater sources. It reports ambient-air compliance, but also surface-water chromium and groundwater aluminium exceedances. Its broad groundwater-compliance conclusion therefore needs reconciliation. This is a proponent's assessment hosted by OSPCB, not an independent regulator finding. The underlying laboratory tables remain unchecked. [EIA summary, pp.8–10](../sources/mining-sukurangi-eia-summary.md).

Do not turn a seasonal study into a present-day drinking-water guarantee. Keep pollutant species, source type, sampling point, detection limit and applicable standard together. The existing [city air-quality series](../statistics/air-quality.md) has different geography and coverage and cannot establish this mine's contribution to pollution.

A [reproduced July 2026 NGT order](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/in/6a844d3b979ea4201d807c32) is a follow-up lead for wider Sukinda monitoring and remedial action. Its original judgment and study annexures still need retrieval. No numerical table from the mirror enters the atlas. Keep residents' allegations, medical evidence, regulator measurements and tribunal findings distinct.

## Niyamgiri: rights are part of the decision

The Supreme Court's 18 April 2013 judgment required Gram Sabhas to examine affected forest and cultural/religious claims, including worship rights. MoEF was then to decide Stage-II clearance in light of their decisions. A District-Judge-rank observer was to certify independent proceedings. This is a specific rights process, not an automatic approval or a blanket statewide veto. [Official SCR edition, paragraphs 58–63](../sources/mining-niyamgiri-scr2013.md).

The OMC proposal in this case is distinct from Baphlimali/Utkal Alumina. Signed village resolutions, observer certifications and the subsequent ministry decision are still needed to complete this historical decision chain. A judgment describing a process does not prove how every later step occurred.

## Bharatpur: follow the recommendation into action

DGMS's May 2024 alert concerns a 14 March 2023 accident at MCL's Bharatpur coal mine in Angul. A contractual tipper operator died after a collision with an abandoned building at a road curve; the alert reports excessive speed. Its prevention recommendations cover transport checks, contractor training, road obstructions and signage. [DGMS, pp.1–2](../sources/mining-dgms-bharatpur-2024.md).

The next question is whether the site implemented those measures. The alert alone cannot answer it. Nor can one incident supply an annual total or accident rate without a complete register and exposure denominator. The study records the worker's role without reproducing unnecessary personal details or accident photographs.

## Baphlimali: planted land is not yet a recovery trend

Reuse the [existing mine and annual-return studies](bauxite-mine-identities.md). They preserve different dated measures of reclaimed, backfilled, afforested and rehabilitated land; these cannot form one trend.

The January 2026 ASI audit rates rehabilitation criterion 8.7 as conforming, while air criterion 6.1 records a monitoring gap for mine vehicles. Neither the mine-scoped certificate nor its rehabilitation finding establishes final closure or ecological recovery. The refinery is outside its scope. [Audit, pp.10–11,13–14](../sources/mining-asi-baphlimali2026.md).

The planting-count wording is ambiguous, and the reported survival percentage lacks a cohort/sampling basis in the public summary. Do not relabel the count as independently verified species or seedlings. Obtain the underlying rehabilitation audit, map comparable areas, and examine survival, soil, water, biodiversity and post-mining use before describing recovery. Corrective-action closure remains unverified.

## A reusable case-study model

|Question|Record to retain|What it cannot establish alone|
|---|---|---|
|Which site and people?|Mine identity, operator, affected settlements, dated boundary|All nearby mines have the same impact|
|What was required?|Permit condition or exact decision paragraph and date|Compliance or present permission|
|What was measured?|Station/sample, medium, method, units, season, denominator|Exposure or health causation|
|Whose finding is it?|Company disclosure, regulator measurement, court finding, community testimony|Independent corroboration merely because a government site hosts a file|
|What changed?|Matched before/after observations and corrective-action evidence|Recovery from a plan, expense or planting count|
|What remains unresolved?|Named missing record, scope conflict and next check|Failure or success from the absence of a record|

A future public narrative should connect these records to consented local voices. It should not invent testimony, collapse communities into a single opinion or use a celebratory industrial image as evidence of an outcome. No outreach or interviews were performed for this batch.

Below detection is not zero, and a dated sampled result is not a current drinking-water assurance. The original Sukurangi EIA laboratory-table gap remains separate.
