# Siali: leaves, fibre and a distinct plant identity

**Siali needs a separate identity from sal.** IISc's Eastern Ghats flora places Siali and Sialpatra under *Phanera vahlii*, retaining *Bauhinia vahlii* as another taxonomic name. It describes a liana and records Odisha localities including Koraput and the Similipal hills. This is a botanical locality reference, not a harvest or enterprise census. [IISc](https://indiaflora-ces.iisc.ac.in/EasternGhats/herbsheet.php?cat=4&id=1830).

## Separate material and use

- **Leaves:** Vasundhara's previously saved livelihood account lists siali leaves among traded products. Record leaf collection, stitching, processing and sales as separate questions; current output and prices remain unknown.
- **Fibre:** SCSTRTI's historical Mankirdia account describes siali-fibre ropes, slings, nets, bags and small baskets (printed p.32). Attribute this account to that community and source, not every Odisha household.
- **Seeds:** IISc notes food use, but does not establish a local recipe, present market or safe identification procedure for a reader. Keep seeds distinct from leaf and fibre commodities.

## Names prevent misleading stories

The flora also lists “Mahul” among Hindi names for siali. A similar-looking name must not merge it with [mahua/mohula](../food/mahua-mohula.md), whose botanical reference is *Madhuca*. Likewise, [Mohu](../food/mohu-honey.md) means honey in the user's clarification. Local names need language and plant-part fields.

The local accounts below begin to document leaf-plate manufacture, maker attribution and seasonality. Present operations, wider raw-material sourcing and environmental comparisons remain research questions. Material being plant-based alone does not prove sustainable harvesting, a particular life-cycle footprint or measured exports.

## From leaf collection to a finished plate

The Balliguda working plan for **2021–22 to 2030–31** describes siali leaves being sun-dried for **two to three days**, then joined with bamboo slivers: **five to six leaves per plate**, packed in bundles of **80 plates**. These are a division account's process and packing conventions, not a universal standard or measured factory output. It identifies **October–February** as the local peak for leaf quality and availability. [Plan, printed pp.198–199](https://odishaforest.in/admin/data/documents/div_working_plan_file_831468672.pdf).

## A named maker and a different local calendar

CYSD's February–April **2002** fieldwork records **Rajani Majhi of Satajhari, Nayagarh**, collecting forest leaves with other women. Her case describes machine sewing for siali and bamboo-stick hand stitching for sal. Its account places abundant leaf supply in **May–January**, poor sewing suitability in **February–April**, and greater priority for agriculture in **July–September**. The seasonal paragraph follows the siali production account, but the household handles both leaves; its exact species scope remains qualified. Forest degradation is reported as a concern. This is published testimony, not a new interview. [NRI Report 2714, printed p.26](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/57a08d48e5274a31e0001784/cysd-orissa-2714.pdf).

Do not average the Nayagarh and Balliguda windows into an Odisha calendar: they differ in place, period, species precision and whether they describe broad availability or peak quality.

## The skills between forest and dining table

JICA's **February 2017** survey describes a Kandhamal chain from collection and stitching through drying, bundling, trading and further trimming or pressing. It distinguishes hand, sewing-machine and press-moulded products. This gives us a useful editorial connection between forest knowledge, women's work, craft and food service. It supplies no current statewide employment or export total here. [Attachment 6.9.3-13–14](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12284139_02.pdf).

[Sal leaves and seeds](sal-products.md) explains the separate tree, material uses and local collection prescription. The leaf-plate story deserves its own craft and livelihood context; environmental superiority still needs a measured comparison.
