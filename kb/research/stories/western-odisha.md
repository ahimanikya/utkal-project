# Western Odisha, beyond the familiar refrain

A song can arrive before you do. You might recognise “Rangabati” long before you can place its makers on a map. Following those names offers a different beginning for a journey through western Odisha: listen first, then ask what lies around the music.

Here is one detail to carry with you. In a January 2022 interview in *Odisha Review*, lyricist **Mitrabhanu Gountia** describes the familiar duet as the last part of a three-song radio sequence. He recalls a female solo, a male solo and the duet, and says the disc recording left out the two solos. What many listeners know, in his account, is a piece of a larger telling.

Let that be the invitation for this journey. Give the region time to open beyond the part you already know.

## Begin with the gathering

Sambalpur district’s cultural account describes **Nuakhai** through the first grains of paddy, offerings to deities, an elder sharing new rice with younger family members, and greetings. It also describes **Nuakhai Bhet Ghat** as a gathering of family and friends. These details connect food with relationships: who serves, who returns, who greets whom.

The district places the festival generally in August or September. A particular year’s observances and public events still need checking. Its broad account cannot tell you how every household celebrates, or turn a family occasion into something a visitor can book.

If you receive an invitation, let your host explain the occasion. Ask what the new rice means to them, what they call the dishes they have chosen, and which moments they would prefer to keep private. Come ready to learn a family’s version. Leave room for a different answer in the next home.

You can begin that kind of food conversation outside a festival too. Ask a willing cook about a preparation they associate with the season. Confirm the ingredients of the particular serving if you have dietary needs. A useful food memory can start with one carefully answered question.

## Hear the people behind the song

The 2022 interview, conducted by **Dr Akshaya Kumar Panda**, credits “Rangabati” to Gountia’s lyrics, **Prabhudatta Pradhan’s** composition and the voices of **Jitendriya Haripal** and **Krishna Patel**. Gountia also recalls Gangadhar Meher’s words about the mother tongue inspiring his own writing in Sambalpuri.

Those are the interview’s credits and recollections. They give you people to follow, without making one song a complete account of the region’s languages or communities.

Choose an authorised recording whose version and creators you can identify. Listen once for the exchange of voices; listen again for what you missed. If a speaker helps you understand a phrase, ask how they would explain it in context. Keep their explanation distinct from a published translation. A playlist becomes more interesting when the names behind it stay attached.

## Follow a literary name to Barpali

**Gangadhar Meher** brings another doorway into view. Sambalpur district’s biographical note places his birth in a weaving family at **Barpali, now in Bargarh district**, and remembers him as Swabhaba Kabi. Among the works it lists are *Pranaya Ballari*, *Indumati* and *Krushaka Sangita*.

A birthplace can change the way you look at a map. Barpali becomes somewhere to begin a literary inquiry: which edition would a local reader put in your hands, which poem would they return to, which image would they ask you to notice?

Start with an accessible edition before planning a special visit. Record its editor or translator and read beyond a borrowed quotation. We have not selected a poem excerpt for this article, nor confirmed access to a house, memorial or library.

## Look beyond the view

At [Debrigarh and Hirakud](../places/debrigarh-hirakud.md), there is also a human story to read beside the landscape. The Prime Minister’s 30 August 2026 *Mann Ki Baat* address names **Maithili Bhue of Dhodrokusum**, describing her involvement in Debrigarh ecotourism. It presents a shift in her relationship with the forest and refers to women taking part in conservation and tourism work.

That is a published official account, not our interview with Bhue or a measurement of household earnings. It gives a name to follow and a question to keep: whose work makes a nature visit possible?

When considering an experience, ask the operator who guides it, what local work it supports, and what conduct the place requires. Check the actual activity, access conditions and availability. Leave space for quiet observation without treating a wildlife sighting as something owed to you.

## Give the journey a centre

Choose the experience you most want to understand: a public cultural gathering, a reading visit, a confirmed workshop, or a nature outing. Build the practical plan around that choice. These pages connect ideas; they do not put every stop next door to the next, or promise a single day will hold them all.

Make a small notebook before you travel: one work to read, one recording to identify, one question for a willing host. Add the answers with their names and permission. You may return with a better understanding of a song you already loved—and with a reason to return for the parts you have yet to hear.
