# Odisha Roots: four investigations into belonging

Four complete narrative drafts follow the Pattachitra story. Each has source notes and a companion evidence record. Together they explore how a birthplace, a sacred name, an institution and an administrative language become questions of belonging.

## 4. Jayadeva and the places that remember him

[Read the story](jayadeva-places-that-remember.md) · [Evidence](jayadeva-places-that-remember.json)

A later manuscript colophon opens the enquiry. The research expands the familiar Odisha–Bengal disagreement to include a Mithila claim, and separates literary association, archaeology and ritual reception. The poet’s birthplace remains unresolved by this investigation.

**Next evidence that would matter:** the Kenduli excavation report and person-specific identification, manuscript witnesses and an epigraphic edition of the Puri record.

## 5. Puri, Digha and the meaning of Dham

[Read the story](puri-digha-meaning-of-dham.md) · [Evidence](puri-digha-meaning-of-dham.json)

An official inauguration notice establishes historical name use; reported statements explain the competing meanings attached to it. The ending includes the June 2026 announcement rather than leaving the dispute frozen in 2025.

**Next evidence that would matter:** the original correspondence, renaming order and dated evidence of implementation. A reported decision and a completed administrative change remain distinct.

## 6. Mayurbhanj Chhau and the question of recognition

[Read the story](mayurbhanj-chhau-recognition.md) · [Evidence](mayurbhanj-chhau-recognition.json)

The strongest new primary-source discovery is a parliamentary objection about the location of a national centre. A later official training record broadens the enquiry to gurus, regional provision and institutional support. This updates the earlier shortlist, which had established shared heritage but no specific dispute.

**Next evidence that would matter:** the institutional decision trail and practitioners’ accounts of training, fees and opportunities. Neither a parliamentary origin claim nor UNESCO recognition proves exclusive ownership.

## 7. Sambalpur and the language of public life

[Read the story](sambalpur-language-public-life.md) · [Evidence](sambalpur-language-public-life.json)

Newspapers and a delegation bring people into a history of administrative language and changing boundaries. The story distinguishes the language campaign from a contemporary interstate accusation and preserves uncertainty around exact notification dates.

**Next evidence that would matter:** original orders, petitions and newspaper issues, especially voices less visible in later commemorative histories.
