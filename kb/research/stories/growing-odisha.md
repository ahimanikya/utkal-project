# Growing Odisha: numbers with a human setting

**See what is taking shape.**

What would make Odisha’s growth feel real to you? A steadier order book? More reliable work? A business that can reach its buyers without losing time or money along the way?

Those are personal questions. A state-level number cannot answer them alone, but it can point towards an activity worth understanding. Start with three different measures: fish produced, cargo handled and value added by construction. Each opens a door. The next part of the story asks who is on the other side.

## Follow the fish beyond the total

The Odisha Economic Survey 2025–26 reports fish production rising from **469.5 thousand tonnes in 2014–15 to 1,191.8 thousand tonnes in 2024–25**. That is a calculated **153.8% increase** over ten years. The [original comparison](../economy/comparison-01-fisheries.md) preserves this interval.

A later Department of Fisheries release, dated **9 July 2026**, reports **12.70 lakh tonnes for 2025–26**. Of that year’s reported components, freshwater accounts for 8.27 lakh tonnes. The sea is only one part of the production picture. The [fisheries record](../statistics/fisheries.md) keeps the newer release and its rounded components separate from the earlier survey table.

Now ask what a larger harvest means for someone trying to earn a living from it. What price did they receive? What did feed, fuel, ice or transport cost in their particular operation? Was there a buyer when the product was ready? These are questions for future reporting, not answers supplied by the production total.

A good feature could follow one consignment with a willing producer and document the steps between production and payment. That would give the reader a human setting for the statistic while leaving room for an experience that differs from the statewide trend. No such participant account has yet been collected for this article.

## A port measures movement

**Paradip handled 156.45 million tonnes of cargo in FY2025–26**, according to the Port Authority’s 1 April 2026 release. A 2017 Shipping Ministry reply records **76.39 million tonnes for FY2015–16**. The endpoint comparison is a calculated **104.8% increase**—more than twice the cargo over a decade.

One detail changes how that headline should be read. The port reports **65.81 million tonnes of coastal cargo**, representing **42.06%** of its FY2025–26 total. Its activity includes domestic coastal movement as well as overseas trade and serves a wider hinterland. These tonnes cannot all be described as Odisha-made exports. Our [Paradip evidence record](../trade/paradip-throughput.md) keeps that distinction visible.

For an entrepreneur, the useful next question may concern the journey to the ship: which service is needed, who supplies it, and where does a delay occur? For someone living near a transport corridor, the questions may concern access, working conditions or environmental effects. Cargo throughput provides a scale for that inquiry; it does not settle any of those experiences.

The port’s published expansion plans belong in a different part of the story. An expected completion date remains a plan until there is evidence that the facility is operating. A photograph of construction would not close that gap by itself.

## Keep the dip in the picture

Odisha’s construction **real gross state value added rose 62.2% between FY2019–20 and FY2024–25**, calculated from the survey’s constant-2011–12-price series. It fell in FY2020–21 before rising. Here is the full six-year interval, in **₹ crore**:

| Financial year | Construction real value added |
| --- | ---: |
| 2019–20 | 25,976.11 |
| 2020–21 | 23,648.15 |
| 2021–22 | 29,950.62 |
| 2022–23 | 32,934.07 |
| 2023–24 · second revised estimate | 38,465.22 |
| 2024–25 · first revised estimate | 42,144.69 |

The survey table is in ₹ lakh; the values above divide by 100. This measures economic value added after allowing for price changes. It is not a count of jobs or a total of public infrastructure spending. The [construction record](../economy/construction.md) retains the series and method.

Keeping the dip matters to the story. Two endpoints can describe an increase while concealing the uneven journey between them. Someone whose work depends on that activity may remember the difficult year as clearly as the later recovery. Finding out how requires listening, rather than assigning a memory to an unnamed worker.

## Bring the question back to people

These three measures are useful together because they ask us to look in different ways. They have different units, periods and coverage, so their growth rates must not be added into a single score. The [GDP story](../economy/gdp-story.md) provides a separate introduction to the economy’s overall scale.

For the next reported feature, choose one activity and one question that a participant wants to explain. Keep permission, attribution and the period of their account with the evidence. Then ask whether the wider number helps the reader understand that account—or whether it needs to stay in the background.

Odisha’s development deserves both kinds of attention: the scale visible in a table and the detail that becomes clear only when someone tells you how their work has changed. The ambition of *Growing Odisha* is to bring them together without making either speak for the other.
