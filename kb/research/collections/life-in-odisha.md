# Life in Odisha

What does access to learning, care, work and everyday services look like? Start with one measure at a time, keep the date in view, and follow it to the questions that matter for people’s lives. These are statewide observations, not a city liveability ranking.

## Learning: the scale of the school system

**61,565 schools** — Schools. Academic year 2024–25; Odisha.[^people-udise2025]

The administrative return covers all school management types in the reporting system. Count schools alongside learning outcomes, attendance, staffing and the reliability of facilities; institution counts alone do not measure education quality.

## Health: a broader reach of financial coverage

**47.9% → 82.7% of households** with at least one usual member covered by health insurance or a financing scheme, comparing NFHS-5 (2019–21) as reproduced in the NFHS-6 release with NFHS-6 (2023–24). Odisha’s latter fieldwork ran **18 July–28 November 2024**.[^people-nfhs6]

This is a survey comparison. It does not mean every household member was insured, that every claim was paid or that out-of-pocket costs disappeared. The [health evidence](../statistics/health.md) also retains maternal and child-service measures, including near-flat observations.

## Connection: a dated subscription count

**30.56 million subscriptions** — Internet subscriptions. 2026-03-31; Odisha.[^weekly-trai-mar2026]

Subscriptions are not unique people. This counts wired and wireless, broadband and narrowband connections; it does not measure speed, affordability or reliability. We do not compute an unqualified growth rate against the earlier release because some earlier operator data were carried forward.
