# Nandankanan: wildlife, conservation and a public institution

**39.35 lakh visits in 2024–25.** Nandankanan connects a popular day out near Bhubaneswar with breeding programmes, veterinary research, wetland habitats and wildlife education. Its reported revenue was **₹25.55 crore** that financial year, rounded from ₹2,554.79 lakh. Visits are not unique people; receipts are not profit or wider economic impact. [Annual report, printed p.72](https://nandankanan.org/images/documents/annual-report-2024-25.pdf).

## What changed

Against 2023–24, reported visits rose **0.4%**, while nominal revenue rose **18.1%**. Attendance was broadly steady. The report does not establish the cause of the revenue change, and these figures do not measure conservation quality. The structured register retains the full five-year table and calculation inputs.

## Conservation and history

Opened on **29 December 1960**, the institution’s account records captive gharial breeding and three white cubs born to normally coloured tiger parents in **1980**. Its claim to the world’s first captive gharial breeding remains explicitly attributed. It is not a claim to the first white tiger anywhere. [Annual report, history and preface](https://nandankanan.org/images/documents/annual-report-2024-25.pdf).

The [tiger programme page](https://www.nandankanan.org/conservation-breeding-of-tiger.php) dates participation in conservation breeding to 2009 and records a pseudomelanistic cub born on 28 July 2014. These are colour forms of tigers, not additional species. Breeding for a rare appearance is not itself evidence of a healthier wild population.

The annual report records **35 wild gharial hatchlings at Satkosia on 24 May 2024**. The [2025 highlights](https://nandankanan.org/images/documents/HIGHLIGHTS-2025.pdf) separately report **seven gharials released into the Mahanadi** and **14 hatchlings in the zoo’s pool**. Keep wild breeding, captive births and releases separate. The [recovery-project account](https://nandankanan.org/mobile/page.php?pid=gharial-breeding-at-nandankanan) also records entanglement, injury and mortality threats; post-release survival remains a research gap.

## A dated animal collection

The 2025 highlights report **4,110 animals representing 177 species as of 25 December 2025**. These are housed animals and the publisher’s species count, not a census of Odisha wildlife. The brochure calls the collection India’s largest; a like-for-like national inventory comparison is still needed before adopting that ranking.

Its **38.56 lakh visits and ₹23.37 crore revenue as of 25 December 2025** are partial-calendar-year figures. They must not be substituted for the financial-year series or used to claim an annual decline. The report also describes schools programmes and the conservation research centre, giving education and science their own place in this entry.

## Habitats and limits

The annual report places the zoo alongside Kanjia Lake and the State Botanical Garden within the sanctuary landscape. It also records animal deaths, including Nandan and Sara, alongside breeding achievements. A collection-size headline is not a welfare assessment.

The annual report’s preface says animal exchanges introduced 94 animals from 27 species; the website’s Director’s Desk says 30 species. This subtotal is held for reconciliation. All saved publications come from the same institution and are not independent confirmation.
