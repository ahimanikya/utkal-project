# Hirapur: a circle of goddesses

Hirapur in Khordha district offers a distinctive architectural encounter: an open-air circular sanctuary with inward-facing Yogini sculptures. IGNCA’s inventory also records a central mandapa and images of Bhairavas. These details connect goddess worship with Shaiva imagery. [Inventory, pp.1–3](https://ignca.gov.in/asi_reports/orkhurda040.pdf).

The inventory distinguishes **sandstone construction from black-chlorite interior images**. Its sixty wall niches and central group describe an arrangement, not a newly verified count of surviving objects. It records weathering of the outer sculptures, a reminder that conservation belongs in the heritage story too.

Dating remains attributed: the inventory argues for the early tenth century from architectural and sculptural style, whereas the saved Odisha Tourism account says ninth century and names Hiradevi. No foundation inscription was examined here to settle the difference. The inventory’s description of active worship has no stated survey date; current rituals, entry, hours and facilities remain unverified.
