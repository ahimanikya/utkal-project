# Ore production: trends, places and source differences

**Odisha’s bauxite output nearly doubled over ten years in the state Directorate series:** 9.192 million tonnes in 2014–15 became 17.934 million tonnes in 2024–25, a calculated **95.1% increase**. This is ore production, not aluminium output. [State table](https://www.odishaminerals.gov.in/StatisticsReport/DespatchProductionReport).

The evidence now keeps production and despatch separately for five minerals across eleven annual observations. It also retains declines: chromite falls from 3.930 million tonnes in 2019–20 to 3.187 million tonnes in 2024–25 in that state capture, while manganese slips from 0.659 to 0.618 million tonnes in its final year. These attributed series do not settle differences with IBM.

Even the two state website routes differ for 2019–20 iron ore: 146.016 versus 142.159; the existing five-year comparison remains a historical imported claim requiring review.

## Put mineral evidence back in its place

IBM’s 2023–24 bauxite producer table links NALCO and OMC to Koraput, and Utkal Alumina to Rayagada. These are mine-location districts, not office addresses or exact lease identities. Its chromite tables preserve Jajpur and Kendujhar quantities and mark Dhenkanal as labour-only reporting; no output is inferred from a reporting-mine count.

Resource tables are dated **1 April 2020** and separate reserves from remaining resources. For bauxite, 409.740 million tonnes are classified as reserves within 2,057.024 million tonnes total resources; for haematite iron ore the corresponding quantities are 2,798.749 and 9,409.331 million tonnes. Historical inventory does not become current mineable supply. [IBM Yearbook 2024](https://www.ibm.gov.in/writereaddata/files/177426215469c1178a48453IMYB_2024_EBookFinal.pdf).
