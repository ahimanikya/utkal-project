# Odisha’s homestay opportunity

Odisha is building a public support system for family-hosted stays. The strongest evidence today concerns the scheme, its funding envelope and planned expansion. A historical marketplace report also suggests more properties were being advertised, while actual demand, host income and operating-unit growth remain unmeasured in this collection.

The first-cycle report describes 61 Gram Panchayats across 19 districts and 19 clusters, including targets of 60 in Koraput and 50 in Kandhamal. These are selected examples, not a complete district allocation list. They must not be added to the statewide programme target.[^homestay-cycle1-may2026]

## Is popularity increasing?

A January 2025 newspaper report describes roughly 50 properties advertised as homestays in 2017 and over 350 by its publication date. It also counted 270 Airbnb listings, including apartments, villas and other property types. This is a sign of a broader marketed offering, with unclear de-duplication and consistent definitions; it is not a measured statewide booking-growth series.[^homestay-market-jan2025]

The same report describes approximately 100 properties with 250 rooms identified around Bhubaneswar for Pravasi Bharatiya Diwas. That event inventory is separate from permanent registered supply.[^homestay-market-jan2025]

A parliamentary reply of 11 August 2025 lists **14 Odisha rural homestays on NIDHI**, a voluntary national registry. That narrow count neither disproves the marketplace figures nor measures all homestays. The reply expressly says NIDHI does not record tourism or occupancy days.[^homestay-nidhi2025]

## What would demonstrate sustained growth?

Follow the same defined group through applications, approval, construction, commercial operation and annual compliance. Then measure rooms available, room-nights sold, occupancy and host earnings. Keep closures and cancellations. Our [monitoring record](../references/data/homestay-research.json) leaves unavailable outcomes unknown rather than zero.

A useful story now is **“Odisha is investing in family-hosted tourism.”** Claims of an occupancy boom, rising household incomes or a successful 450-property rollout need further evidence.
