# Koraput, Sambalpur and Bargarh: people and work baselines

**Koraput’s recorded literacy rate was 49.21% in Census 2011.** This is a historical measure of residents aged seven and above. It helps describe the setting for later education and livelihoods research; it cannot describe today’s literacy or the skills available to a new business.

## Education and recorded work · Census 2011

| District | Literacy, age 7+ | Male literacy, age 7+ | Female literacy, age 7+ | Workers, main + marginal | Workers / total population |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Koraput | 49.21% | 60.32% | 38.55% | 693,406 | 50.26% |
| Sambalpur | 76.22% | 84.35% | 67.93% | 505,840 | 48.59% |
| Bargarh | 74.62% | 83.68% | 65.38% | 762,092 | 51.45% |

Sources: the respective 2011 District Census Handbooks, published in 2014. “Important Statistics” appears on PDF pages 23 (Koraput), 29 (Sambalpur) and 26 (Bargarh). Literacy denominator explanations appear on PDF pages 27, 33 and 31 respectively. See the original documents in this page’s source metadata and the [observation ledger](../references/data/statistics-atlas.json).

Workers include both main and marginal workers. The percentage uses the entire district population, including children, as its denominator. It is not an adult employment rate, formal-job count, labour-force participation rate or evidence of a workforce presently available for recruitment. The geography is each district at Census 2011, not a city or the older undivided Sambalpur region.

The Ministry of Women and Child Development reproduced these NFHS-5 estimates in the Annexure to [Lok Sabha question 1060, answered 22 July 2022](https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/179/AU1060.pdf?source=pqals), PDF page 3. They are adverse historical findings worth retaining alongside economic stories.

The annexure supplies the national survey-round label, not each district’s exact fieldwork dates, valid-measurement sample sizes or confidence intervals. Those fields remain unknown here. These percentages must not be added together: the indicators can overlap. Do not multiply them by Census 2011 population to estimate affected children, present them as current prevalence, or treat small differences as statistically established rankings.

## Connect people with the local economy

[Koraput](../places/koraput.md) connects coffee, rice, food knowledge and institutions. [Sambalpur–Bargarh](../places/sambalpur-bargarh.md) connects weaving, rice and the wider regional landscape. Read these baselines alongside the [rice economy](../economy/rice-economy.md), [handloom traditions](../handlooms/index.md) and [district banking](district-banking.md). The links describe related subjects; they do not establish that a crop, craft or bank programme caused any measured social outcome.

For a prospective enterprise, the next useful evidence is recent local training and recruitment capacity, household demand, practical infrastructure and sector-specific operating costs. Historical literacy and work counts cannot substitute for those checks.
