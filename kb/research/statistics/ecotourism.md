# Ecotourism: income, visitors and community livelihoods

**Reported programme income rose about 26% to ₹19.12 crore in FY 2024–25**, from ₹15.17 crore in FY 2023–24. The Wildlife Organisation’s 2025 publication supplies the two full-year figures; this is a comparison of reported monetary income, without an established inflation adjustment. It does not measure household earnings.

Visitor footfall increased from 151,517 to 170,365, about 12.4%. The same table records fewer non-Indian visitors: 104 to 76, a fall of 26.9%. These categories describe recorded visitor activity; unique people, nights stayed and occupancy are not established.

## Where local communities fit

The report describes **more than 700 community members managing 53 ecotourism destinations** in its 2025 snapshot. It names work such as cooking, housekeeping, reception and guiding. This is programme participation, not 700 verified permanent jobs or a current 2026 headcount.

Its income-sharing mechanism assigns 35% to community wages/salary, 25% to recurring costs, 10% to infrastructure, 10% to village development through EDCs and 20% to a corpus for hospitality experts, promotion and major maintenance. These are allocation rules. Multiplying the income total by 35% would not prove that amount reached workers; no payroll or payment ledger was obtained.

## A longer view

Reported income increased from ₹6.58 crore in FY 2019–20 to ₹19.12 crore in FY 2024–25, about 190.5% over five years. The programme’s changing destination mix is not controlled here. Keep this as aggregate programme growth rather than growth at the same properties.

The latest row in this publication is **partial FY 2025–26, through 6 September 2025**: ₹5.16 crore and 45,028 recorded visitors. It cannot be compared with a full year to claim a decline or extrapolated into a final result.
