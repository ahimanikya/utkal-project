# Bhubaneswar’s Painted Streets

*A walk, a camera, and a city telling its stories on its walls.*

Photographs and recollections by **Ahimanikya Satapathy** · Bhubaneswar, February 2009

*Image captions are curatorial additions. [Explore their cultural connections and sources](/stories/bhubaneswar-fresco/#references).*

<img src="/stories/bhubaneswar-fresco/photos/IMG_1262.JPG" alt="A long red boundary wall painted with a procession of figures and cattle, beside a brick pavement." width="1600" height="1200" loading="lazy" />

*The painted procession keeps pace with the pavement, turning a walk through Bhubaneswar into a passage through stories.*

[Explore: A city remembers in public](/stories/bhubaneswar-fresco/#references)

During my Odisha visit in February 2009, I came across paintings on the boundary walls along Bhubaneswar’s main streets. I did not know who had painted them. But when I saw them, I couldn’t resist taking photographs.

I spent hours walking, stopping, and photographing. Even after all that walking, I had captured only a fraction of what was there. The next day, I wrote to friends to share my surprise:

> “I recently visited Bhubaneswar, and to my surprise, I found lots of paintings on the street boundary walls. I did capture a fraction of those paintings, and I am sure you will enjoy them...”

— From my email of 19 February 2009

Looking through these photographs now, I notice the pavements and trees as much as the paintings. Shadows fall across the walls. Leaves partly cover a face or a decorative border. These details preserve something of the experience of encountering the art on foot.

There is so much to look at. Small figures gather in rows. Musicians play beside dancers. Animals, trees and birds fill the spaces between them. A painted train carries its own little crowd. Each panel rewards another moment of attention.

Other walls bring craft into view: masks, vessels, toys and detailed scenes of making. One panel even announces its subject in painted lettering: “Our Palm Leaf Work.” Together, the photographs offer many possible starting points for discovering Odisha through its art.

Some paintings spread whole scenes across a wall; others draw the eye to a single ornament. A circular white design against deep red is enough to make me stop again. Seen together, these pictures hold both kinds of pleasure: the large unfolding story and the small detail.

<img src="/stories/bhubaneswar-fresco/photos/IMG_1285.JPG" alt="An intricate circular white floral design painted on a deep red wall." width="1600" height="1200" loading="lazy" />

*A white line circles back to itself, carrying the intimacy of a decorated threshold onto a public wall.*

[Explore: The threshold and the street](/stories/bhubaneswar-fresco/#references)

For me, these photographs are a record of the hours I spent looking. I still associate Bhubaneswar with this culture of art in everyday public spaces. This collection preserves the walls as I encountered them in February 2009.

<img src="/stories/bhubaneswar-fresco/photos/IMG_1299.JPG" alt="A tree-lined Bhubaneswar pavement follows a red wall decorated with white and yellow motifs, with the road visible to the left." width="1600" height="1200" loading="lazy" />

*The trees, period signs and painted wall preserve the ordinary surroundings of an extraordinary walk.*

[Explore: A city remembers in public](/stories/bhubaneswar-fresco/#references)

The artists behind these walls deserve to be remembered too. I did not know their names when I took the photographs. If you recognise a painting, its artist, or the stretch of street where it stood, your recollection could help add another piece to this story.

**About this collection:** Photographed in Bhubaneswar in February 2009. The selected files record 18 February 2009 in their camera metadata; the accompanying email is dated 19 February. Photography: Ahimanikya Satapathy. Mural artists: not yet identified. These are archival photographs; the present condition of the pictured walls has not been checked.

**The story continued:** Bhubaneswar Smart City Limited later documented STAMP, its Street Art and Mural Project, covering murals, installations and street painting. This supplies later context for public art in the city; it does not identify or explain the commissioning of the works in this collection.
